## 1. Scope Boundary & Classification

Module 6 explicitly delineates what is in-scope for analysis versus what is out-of-scope.

### In Scope
1. **Formal Domain Definition ($A_C$):** Characterizing classical algorithm finite transition systems $(D_\text{fin}, R_P)$.
2. **Formal Codomain Definition ($C_Q$):** Characterizing `QuantumCircuitIR` AST space ($C_Q^\text{logical}$).
3. **Compiler Mapping Analysis ($F$):** Formalizing compiler-induced mapping $F: A_C \to C_Q^\text{logical}$.
4. **Mapping Properties Investigation:** Mathematically and empirically evaluating injectivity, surjectivity, bijectivity, and image $\text{Img}(F) \subsetneq C_Q$.
5. **Multi-Level Equivalence Analysis:** Evaluating semantic preservation across 6 distinct equivalence levels (functional, transition, basis, superposition, operator, measurement).
6. **Expressibility & Subset Analysis:** Determining the exact structural and semantic subset of $C_Q$ generated by classical algorithms.
7. **Counterexample Construction:** Documenting and preserving explicit counterexamples (e.g. Hadamard superposition gates) where classical equivalence fails.
8. **Executable Verification:** Implementing automated integration tests to evaluate equivalence claims against empirical compiler outputs.

### Out of Scope
1. **Upstream Compiler Modification:** Altering any source code, contracts, or logic in Modules 1–5.
2. **Hardware Execution & Benchmarking:** Submitting circuits to physical quantum QPUs or vendor APIs (IBM, Google, AWS).
3. **Physical Noise Analysis:** Modeling device noise, decoherence, or pulse-level calibration.
4. **Compiler Redesign:** Modifying compiler transformation rules to force injectivity, surjectivity, or universality.
5. **Unfounded Universality Claims:** Asserting that classical algorithms span the full space of quantum circuits ($C_Q$).
6. **Unfounded Quantum Advantage Claims:** Asserting quantum speedup or advantage merely from having a valid quantum circuit representation.
7. **Unrestricted Infinite Domain Extension:** Silently extending finite-domain results ($D_\text{fin}$) to infinite state spaces.
